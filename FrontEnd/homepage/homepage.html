About Us

WasteNot aims to reduce food waste by helping food providers redistribute safe, edible surplus to NGOs and communities that need it.

  • NGOs can quickly request donations so surplus food reaches communities in need.
  • The system reduces landfill waste and lowers environmental impact.
  • Dashboards track waste reduction, donations, and overall impact.
  • We do this because many Filipinos face hunger while tons of food are wasted daily.
  • WasteNot connects providers, NGOs, and consumers to promote a sustainable, responsible food ecosystem.

Household food waste still exists

~2.95 million tonnes of food are thrown away by Filipino households each year — that's about 26 kg per person annually.

Commonly wasted food products

The three most-wasted foods in Filipino homes: Rice (58%), Vegetables (18%), and Meat (9%).

Scale of waste in urban centers

In Metro Manila alone, an estimated 2,175 tons of food scraps are thrown away daily.

Environmental importance

Reducing food waste globally can cut greenhouse-gas emissions significantly; food loss and waste are estimated to contribute up to 10% of all anthropogenic emissions.

“Every meal we throw away is a missed opportunity to feed someone and a burden on our planet. Let’s rescue food before it becomes waste.”

How does our app works?

🍽️

Local restaurants, bakeries, and cafeterias post surplus items that are still perfectly good to eat but would otherwise go to waste.

🛍️

Shoppers browse affordable surplus food nearby, with filters for location, and expiry date.

💚

Charities and community organizations request available surplus food to distribute to their beneficiaries.

📊

An easy dashboard highlights items nearing expiry, letting providers quickly post them for sale or donation.

📈

Both providers and admins can see how much food was saved, donated, and redistributed—turning waste into measurable change.

Frequently Asked Questions

FAQs for Consumers

Q: Can I use WasteNot to get discounted meals or rescue food?

A: Yes! WasteNot is open for all, you can claim products on the marketplace.

Q: Do I need to pay or sign up as a consumer to browse the site?

A: You can use the site for free. Sign up as a consumer and you can buy discounted meals or surplus products with a lower price.

Q: Is the food safe / hygienic?

A: Yes — providers must indicate food type, expiry, pickup/delivery info.

FAQs for Partners/ Providers

Q: Who can request donations?

A: Registered NGOs, community pantries, shelters, and charitable organizations can request surplus food listed by providers.

Q: How does requesting work?

A: Browse available donation listings → submit request → wait for provider approval → coordinate pickup/delivery → mark as completed → update impact log.

Q: Is there any cost or fee for NGOs to use WasteNot?

A: No — requesting donations is free. You may need to arrange your own transportation or pickup depending on provider agreement.

Q: What qualifies as “surplus food” that I can post?

A: Surplus food includes still-edible meals, baked goods, near-expiry ingredients, or prepared food that otherwise would be discarded — as long as it's safe, properly stored, and within regulatory standards.

Q: How does dynamic pricing work?

A: On each listing, you enter expiry date/time. As the expiry approaches, our system automatically reduces the price (or marks items free) to encourage quicker claims or donations.

Q: Do I have to pay for disposal if I donate instead?

A: No — by posting surplus via WasteNot, you're avoiding disposal costs, possibly recovering part of the cost through discounted sales, and contributing to your sustainability/CSR image.

Q: Can I get reports or analytics on how much I’ve saved or donated?

A: Yes — the Impact Report page provides full data (food saved, donated items, estimated waste diverted, etc.) and export options (CSV or PDF).
